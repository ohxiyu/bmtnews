    Crypto lost $110M to hacks in July, Immunefi data shows

    According to Immunefi, the crypto ecosystem lost roughly $110 million to hacks in July, while its audit competitions reportedly outperformed traditional tier-1 audits in finding vulnerabilities. The aggregate loss highlights persistent security risks in DeFi and the broader crypto market. It also suggests that competitive auditing models such as audit competitions may be becoming more effective at identifying vulnerabilities than conventional top-tier audits. Immunefi is a leading Web3 bug bounty platform, and its audit competitions are time-bound code reviews conducted by security researchers. The report compares these competitions with tier-1 audits, which are typically conducted by well-known firms.

    Coldcard Seed-Phrase Hack Proves More Damaging Than Typical Crypto Breach

    On July 30, 2026, a firmware vulnerability in Coldcard hardware wallets allowed attackers to drain roughly 1,082.65 BTC (about $70 million, with later estimates rising to $89 million) from over 1,000 addresses in a 41-minute sweep. Unlike typical hacks, this incident compromised seed phrases directly, making it far more damaging to users' ability to protect their funds. Hardware wallets are widely considered the most secure way to self-custody bitcoin, so a seed-phrase leak undermines the core promise of cold storage and threatens trust in the entire hardware-wallet ecosystem. Affected users must move funds to a newly generated seed phrase, and the industry may need to rethink supply-chain and firmware assurance. According to Bitcoin Magazine, the flaw also affects ephemeral keys, session keys in Clone Coldcard or Key Teleport features, and BIP 85 seeds derived from a compromised seed. Galaxy Research traced the July 30 sweep of 1,196 Bitcoin addresses and tied it to the firmware flaw; Bitcoin Magazine reports that roughly 1,000 BTC connected to the vulnerability has moved on-chain, and attacks are still ongoing.

    Bybit Secures US Asset Freeze in $1.5B Lazarus Hack Lawsuit

    Bybit filed a civil lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against North Korea, its Reconnaissance General Bureau, and the Lazarus Group over the $1.5 billion theft in February 2025, and secured a preliminary injunction freezing identified stolen assets. The court found Bybit likely to succeed on the merits. This is a major legal escalation in the largest crypto exchange hack, potentially setting a precedent for how exchanges can use civil courts and asset freezes to pursue state-sponsored hackers. It also demonstrates growing cooperation between the crypto industry, regulators, and law enforcement in tracing and recapturing stolen funds. Bybit reported that approximately $48.4 million has been recovered and about $30.5 million frozen across more than 28 exchanges and custodians, totaling roughly 5% of the stolen amount. The attackers drained about 500,000 ETH by manipulating a signing interface, and most of the stolen Ethereum was converted to Bitcoin via Thorchain and mixed through Wasabi, Tornado Cash, and Railgun.
