    Bybit Secures US Asset Freeze in $1.5B Lazarus Hack Lawsuit

    Bybit filed a civil lawsuit in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia against North Korea, its Reconnaissance General Bureau, and the Lazarus Group over the $1.5 billion theft in February 2025, and secured a preliminary injunction freezing identified stolen assets. The court found Bybit likely to succeed on the merits. This is a major legal escalation in the largest crypto exchange hack, potentially setting a precedent for how exchanges can use civil courts and asset freezes to pursue state-sponsored hackers. It also demonstrates growing cooperation between the crypto industry, regulators, and law enforcement in tracing and recapturing stolen funds. Bybit reported that approximately $48.4 million has been recovered and about $30.5 million frozen across more than 28 exchanges and custodians, totaling roughly 5% of the stolen amount. The attackers drained about 500,000 ETH by manipulating a signing interface, and most of the stolen Ethereum was converted to Bitcoin via Thorchain and mixed through Wasabi, Tornado Cash, and Railgun.

    Bybit Expands Platform to Offer Stocks, ETFs, and Other Assets

    Bybit has announced an expansion of its trading platform to include stocks, ETFs, and other assets beyond cryptocurrencies. The news was reported by Coinfomania, but specific details on the available instruments or launch timeline have not been disclosed. This move reflects the growing trend of crypto exchanges expanding into traditional financial assets, potentially broadening their user base and increasing trading volumes. It also positions Bybit to compete more directly with multi-asset platforms like Robinhood and eToro. The original article does not specify which stocks, ETFs, or other assets will be offered, nor does it provide a timeline or list of supported jurisdictions. Regulatory scrutiny may become a challenge, given that Bybit has faced warnings from financial regulators in multiple jurisdictions.

    Audited badges misleading crypto investors, Bybit hack shows

    A new analysis argues that crypto 'audited' badges give investors a false sense of security, using Bybit's $1.46 billion exploit to show that audit findings miss non-code attack vectors. It cites research indicating that only a fraction of exploit losses originate in the code that conventional audits inspect. Because blockchain projects use audit badges as marketing signals, investors often equate 'audited' with 'safe,' which can distort risk perception and capital allocation across DeFi. The analysis may accelerate industry pressure for clearer audit scope labeling and stronger operational security practices. The article details that Bybit's Feb. 21, 2025 exploit involved a manipulated signing interface during a routine ETH transfer, after Safe revealed a compromised developer machine; external researchers found no vulnerability in Safe's contracts or front-end source. It also cites an Oak Security preprint that compared 23,818 audit findings with 218 rekt.news incidents representing $7.764 billion in losses, showing many incidents fall outside audited code.
