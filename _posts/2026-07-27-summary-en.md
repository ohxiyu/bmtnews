This marks the first known prosecution involving the use of a duress password at a US border. This case sets a legal precedent for the use of duress PINs and other security features that can automatically destroy data, raising important questions about the balance between individual privacy rights and government authority at borders. It could deter security-conscious users from utilizing such features. The duress PIN on GrapheneOS is designed to wipe the device when entered under coercion, but prosecutors argue the act of wiping constitutes obstruction of justice. The outcome could hinge on whether using a duress PIN is considered an intentional act to destroy evidence.

US border authorities have broad search powers, and individuals are generally expected to comply with device searches. This incident highlights the legal risks of using anti-forensic tools at borders.
