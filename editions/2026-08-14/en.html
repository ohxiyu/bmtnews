Coinbase's Base Abandons Optimistic Rollups, Bets $12B on ZK Proofs

Anthropic Quietly Watermarks New Claude Outputs; Builders Race to Strip It

Anthropic has begun embedding an imperceptible, model-level watermark into text generated by its newest Claude models, effective for models launched in the EU on or after August 2, 2026, and applied worldwide across the Claude chatbot, API, Claude Code, and cloud partners including AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Foundry. The rollout follows Anthropic's signing of the EU AI Act's Code of Practice on transparency obligations. This is one of the first large-scale, model-level watermark rollouts by a major AI lab, touching every Claude user, developer, and enterprise customer worldwide. It could set an industry precedent for how provenance and transparency rules under the EU AI Act are implemented, and how the market reacts to hidden, unverifiable labeling techniques. Anthropic has not yet published the detection method, thresholds, or the exact technique; researchers infer it is a statistical token-selection bias similar to Google's SynthID Text. The watermark survives copy-paste and some editing, and files also carry C2PA signed metadata, but heavy editing can strip it, and a missing watermark does not prove human authorship.

rss · Decrypt · Aug 14, 04:30 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Background

Text watermarking is a technique that embeds hidden, machine-readable information inside content to verify its origin, ownership, or authenticity, and with generative AI it is increasingly used to flag AI-produced text. The EU AI Act's Code of Practice on marking and labelling of AI-generated content is a voluntary framework guiding generative-AI providers toward compliance with transparency obligations, which Anthropic has signed. Google's SynthID is a comparable existing system that watermarks AI-generated text, images, audio, and video. Anthropic's history sharpens the privacy reaction: in March, the company removed a hidden tracker in Claude Code after researchers discovered undisclosed Unicode markers that tagged some users' location and proxy use.

Discussion

Reaction from the builder community has been quick and adversarial: open-source tools such as guillaumemeyer/watermarks-remover (4.6k stars) and mikiane/claude-watermark-cleaner (106 stars) appeared within days to strip or rewrite watermarked content. Critics argue that a statistical text watermark is 'not a reliable way to prove origin' and that because Anthropic has not shipped its detector or thresholds, no removal technique can be verified as complete.

Coinbase and 14 other x402 facilitators failed security tests built for the coming AI-agent economy

Researchers testing 15 major x402 payment facilitators at the USENIX Security Symposium found that every platform, including Coinbase, Thirdweb, PayAI and Mogami, violated at least one security rule. They mapped 49 rule violations to 31 distinct vulnerabilities across systems handling 99% of observed x402 transactions and 98% of payment volume during the study. x402 is being promoted as infrastructure for machine-driven commerce, allowing AI agents to autonomously pay for services, yet these flaws could leave merchants unpaid for goods they have already delivered and expose facilitator-held assets and network fees. The findings highlight systemic risks in the emerging AI-agent payment economy and could affect trust in stablecoin-based automated payments. Researchers identified four broad attack classes (free shopping, asset theft, service disruption, and gas abuse) and directly validated six attack paths, including two free-shopping attacks, three gas-abuse attacks, and one path that could expose facilitator-held assets via malicious ERC-6492 metadata. They also analyzed 119 million x402 transactions on Base and Solana, finding facilitators spent about $202,000 on network fees, including roughly $5,800 on reverted or failed Base transactions.

Background

x402 is an open-source payment protocol launched by Coinbase in May 2025 that enables instant stablecoin payments directly over HTTP, allowing APIs, apps, and AI agents to transact autonomously. Facilitators sit between buyers and merchants, verifying signed payment authorizations before submitting transactions to blockchains, which concentrates settlement control and risk.
