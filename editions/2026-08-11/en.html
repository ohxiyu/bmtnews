Anthropic Proves Over Two-Thirds of Riemann Zeta Zeros Lie on Critical Line

Anthropic has published a mathematical result proving that more than two-thirds of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function lie on the critical line. This marks a significant quantitative advance in the study of one of mathematics' most famous open problems. This is significant because the Riemann hypothesis, which conjectures that all nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line, is one of the most important unsolved problems in pure mathematics. A proof that more than two-thirds of the zeros satisfy the condition is a meaningful step, and the involvement of a leading AI lab highlights the growing role of AI in mathematical research. The result focuses on the proportion of nontrivial zeros on the critical line — the line where the real part equals 1/2. This is a quantitative strengthening of previous estimates, though it does not prove the full Riemann hypothesis.

rss · Anthropic News · Aug 11, 01:52 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Background

The Riemann zeta function, denoted ζ(s), is a function of a complex variable that is central to analytic number theory. Its nontrivial zeros are believed to all lie on the critical line where Re(s)=1/2, which is the Riemann hypothesis; this conjecture has far-reaching implications for the distribution of prime numbers. Establishing that a large fraction of the zeros lie on the line is a known approach to making partial progress.

This signals a major strategic shift for Ethereum as it prepares for "Q-Day" and the potential threat of quantum computers, and it could reshape how the network handles privacy and scaling. Because Buterin is a core figure, this roadmap helps align the ecosystem's long-term research and development priorities, potentially affecting developers, validators, and users. The "Strawmap" deprioritizes VDFs and most EVM improvement work, and drops Verkle trees and state expiry in favor of new constructions. Buterin noted that Ethereum is increasingly relying on STARKs and AI-assisted formal verification to ensure protocol correctness, and he reiterated his "Lean Ethereum" vision of a quantum-safe, privacy-first, censorship-resistant, and performant protocol.

Coldcard Seed-Phrase Hack Proves More Damaging Than Typical Crypto Breach

On July 30, 2026, a firmware vulnerability in Coldcard hardware wallets allowed attackers to drain roughly 1,082.65 BTC (about $70 million, with later estimates rising to $89 million) from over 1,000 addresses in a 41-minute sweep. Unlike typical hacks, this incident compromised seed phrases directly, making it far more damaging to users' ability to protect their funds. Hardware wallets are widely considered the most secure way to self-custody bitcoin, so a seed-phrase leak undermines the core promise of cold storage and threatens trust in the entire hardware-wallet ecosystem. Affected users must move funds to a newly generated seed phrase, and the industry may need to rethink supply-chain and firmware assurance. According to Bitcoin Magazine, the flaw also affects ephemeral keys, session keys in Clone Coldcard or Key Teleport features, and BIP 85 seeds derived from a compromised seed. Galaxy Research traced the July 30 sweep of 1,196 Bitcoin addresses and tied it to the firmware flaw; Bitcoin Magazine reports that roughly 1,000 BTC connected to the vulnerability has moved on-chain, and attacks are still ongoing.

Background

A seed phrase is a sequence of 12–24 random words that acts as the master key to a cryptocurrency wallet; anyone who has it can move the funds.
