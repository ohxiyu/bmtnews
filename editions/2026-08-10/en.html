Hyperliquid RWA perps boom erodes revenue backing HYPE

Hyperliquid's open interest hit a record above $11 billion and 30-day volume reached nearly $178 billion, yet gross protocol revenue fell for a fourth straight quarter to about $202 million in Q2 2026. The gap stems from HIP-3 fee sharing, which now routes roughly half of perp volume through builder-deployed markets that keep up to half the trading fees. This reveals a structural shift in Hyperliquid's economics: record usage is increasingly unprofitable for the protocol because fee-sharing funnels revenue to external builders. Since the Assistance Fund's HYPE buyback is a fixed share of earnings, shrinking revenue means less HYPE is retired, directly weakening the earnings story that underpins token valuation. RWA perps on assets like gold, crude oil, Nvidia, Tesla, the Nasdaq-100 and pre-IPO SpaceX reached a record $3.6 billion in open interest and overtook bitcoin as Hyperliquid's largest perp market. However, Trade.xyz accounts for over 90% of HIP-3 open interest, and a single trade on a thin Korean pre-market venue dropped its SK Hynix contract 19% on Monday, triggering reimbursements.

rss · CoinDesk · Aug 9, 23:00 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The revenue pass-through directly shrinks the Assistance Fund, which buys and retires HYPE on the open market — HYPE buybacks fell from about $290 million in Q3 2025 to roughly $149 million in Q2 2026. Lower buyback-driven token demand, combined with concentrated dependency on one builder's risk infrastructure and a roughly 70x fully diluted earnings multiple, heightens sensitivity to any further revenue compression from fee-sharing.

Background

RWA perps are perpetual futures that give leveraged long or short exposure to real-world assets such as commodities, equities, forex and indices, settled in stablecoins without expiry or physical delivery. HIP-3, launched in October 2025, lets anyone staking 500,000 HYPE deploy their own perpetual market on Hyperliquid's order book and keep up to half the trading fees. Open interest measures the total value of open leveraged positions, while volume tracks total trading activity.

Crypto Dot-Com Shakeout: Over 100 Projects Fold in 2026

Over 100 crypto projects have shut down, filed for bankruptcy, or gone permanently dark in 2026, with four major firms opening closures within a single week in late July, including BitMEX, BitMart, Movement Labs, and Storj Labs. The shakeout is hitting crowded sectors like layer-2 networks and protocol tooling as altcoin prices drop 70% to 90% and token treasuries drain. This marks a maturity shift in crypto, from speculative token distribution to sustainable, cash-flow-positive business models. Projects that charge real fees in stablecoins or cash, like Aave, Hyperliquid, and Ether.fi, are surviving, which signals broader consolidation across DeFi, exchanges, and infrastructure. According to RootData, the pace of shutdowns is accelerating; the exits span exchanges, wallets, DeFi lending protocols, NFT marketplaces, and layer-1 blockchains, including Moonbeam, a Polkadot parachain that shut down on July 31. Over $1.1 billion was lost to exploits in the first half of 2026, and with venture rescue capital drying up, single hacks can force immediate bankruptcies while unmaintained 'zombie contracts' remain on-chain.

rss · CoinDesk · Aug 9, 21:00 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The shakeout concentrates activity and liquidity into fewer protocols and exchanges that generate real cash flow, while zombie contracts and failed projects can create lingering risk for users holding stranded assets. Sectors with heavy token-treasury dependence, such as general-purpose layer-2s and smaller exchanges, face funding and solvency pressure, which can reduce liquidity and raise counterparty risk across the altcoin market.

Background

Layer-2 networks are blockchains built on top of a base chain like Ethereum to process transactions faster and at lower cost; they bundle transactions off-chain and post them back to the main chain. During 2023, advances in this technology made it easy for companies to launch their own layer-2 chains, leading to a crowded market of general-purpose rollups with little differentiation. Token treasuries—reserves of a project's own token—lose value as altcoin prices fall, making it hard for startups to fund operations and eventually forcing closures.

Audited badges misleading crypto investors, Bybit hack shows

A new analysis argues that crypto 'audited' badges give investors a false sense of security, using Bybit's $1.46 billion exploit to show that audit findings miss non-code attack vectors. It cites research indicating that only a fraction of exploit losses originate in the code that conventional audits inspect. Because blockchain projects use audit badges as marketing signals, investors often equate 'audited' with 'safe,' which can distort risk perception and capital allocation across DeFi. The analysis may accelerate industry pressure for clearer audit scope labeling and stronger operational security practices. The article details that Bybit's Feb. 21, 2025 exploit involved a manipulated signing interface during a routine ETH transfer, after Safe revealed a compromised developer machine; external researchers found no vulnerability in Safe's contracts or front-end source. It also cites an Oak Security preprint that compared 23,818 audit findings with 218 rekt.news incidents representing $7.764 billion in losses, showing many incidents fall outside audited code.

rss · CryptoSlate · Aug 9, 17:59 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

This story can affect crypto markets through investor confidence: if prominent audit badges lose credibility, capital may flow more cautiously into DeFi protocols, and projects may face higher perceived risk until they adopt broader security validation. The immediate impact is likely on sentiment and risk pricing around audited tokens and exchanges, rather than on any specific asset price.

Background

Smart-contract audits are finite engagements: auditors review a specific snapshot of code (e.g., a commit hash) over a limited period, and the report does not automatically cover later edits, deployment configuration, or the human and operational layers such as private key management and signing interfaces. The Bybit attack targeted the transaction intent layer that multisig signers rely on, where hardware wallets displayed opaque data and a compromised interface showed a correct address while the underlying logic was altered. This gap between code correctness and operational security is why security researchers argue that audit badges cannot be treated as a general warranty.

Kraken Nears Delisting Deadline for 21 Tokens, Holders Face Near-Zero Payouts

Kraken is approaching its deadline to delist 21 tokens, and holders who fail to act before the cutoff risk receiving payouts worth near zero. The exchange is removing all trading pairs for the affected assets. Delistings on major exchanges like Kraken often trigger sharp price drops because the token loses its primary liquidity venue. This event highlights the risk of holding low-liquidity or non-compliant assets that exchanges may periodically remove. When a token is delisted, all trading pairs are removed, and exchanges typically set a final date after which balances may be converted or liquidated. Affected holders should withdraw tokens to an external wallet before the deadline, as post-deadline payouts can be minimal.

google_news · BeInCrypto · Aug 10, 05:29 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The delisting could compress liquidity and trigger price drops for the 21 affected tokens on Kraken, with possible spillover to other venues as traders exit positions. Broader crypto markets are unlikely to see systemic impact, but affected token holders face direct financial loss as the deadline approaches.

Background

Delisting is the removal of a cryptocurrency from an exchange, meaning the token can no longer be traded on that platform. Exchanges delist assets for reasons such as poor liquidity, low trading volume, lack of development activity, or regulatory concerns. When delisting is announced, the price often collapses because demand disappears with the loss of accessible trading pairs, and holders are encouraged to withdraw funds or close positions before the deadline.
