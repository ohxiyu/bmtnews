Routing Bug Puts Solana Within 4.5 Points of Losing Finality

A misconfigured BGP route at hosting provider Teraswitch took 28.83% of staked SOL offline on Wednesday morning, leaving Solana just 4.51 percentage points short of the 33.34% threshold at which the chain stops finalizing transactions. Roughly 90 validators were affected, and networking recovered after about 33 minutes. This incident is a severe stress test for Solana's network stability: a single provider misconfiguration nearly halted the entire chain, exposing how concentrated staked SOL is within one autonomous system. It underscores risks for validators, staking protocols, and the broader ecosystem, and could intensify calls for better decentralization and failover practices. The affected autonomous system, AS20326, holds roughly 27% of staked SOL — above the 25% cap in the Solana Foundation's delegation program — and 94% of it went offline at once. Marinade Finance found that 80.2 million SOL across 59 validators recovered after routing reconverged, while Helius was down for the full 33 minutes; the 333 SOL in lost rewards will be covered by validator bonds.

rss · Decrypt · Aug 12, 22:13 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The primary exposure is SOL staking infrastructure: approximately 90 validators lost rewards, and staking protocols may tighten delegation-concentration limits. Because finality was never lost, direct on-chain disruption was avoided, but the incident can affect market sentiment about Solana's reliability and decentralization, which feeds into SOL's risk perception.

Background

Solana uses a delegated proof-of-stake mechanism in which validators vote on blocks, and finality requires at least two-thirds of staked SOL to participate. If more than 33.34% of stake goes delinquent (stops voting), the network cannot finalize new blocks. The outage was caused by a BGP default route from Teraswitch's Miami site that was propagated without its metric and communities, making edge routers in Europe and Asia-Pacific treat it as a locally originated route; they then passed an invalid route into data-center cores, leaving 12 sites without a valid path. BGP is the internet's routing protocol, and such misconfigurations can cause large-scale connectivity failures.

Coldcard Hack Spurs $15B Bitcoin Migration to Self-Custody

A Coldcard firmware exploit that began July 30 drained roughly 2,100 BTC (~$130 million), and onchain data shows 233,000 BTC (~$15 billion) moved out of long-term holder wallets in the following days. Casa CEO Nick Neuman attributes part of that migration to Ledger and Trezor users upgrading to multisig self-custody. The incident underscores that hardware wallet vulnerabilities can be contained by distributed self-custody, and may accelerate multisig adoption among long-term Bitcoin holders. It also shows self-custody adapting under stress, reinforcing Bitcoin's resilience as an asset. Checkonchain data shows 233,000 BTC left long-term holder wallets—a 1.38% drop from recent highs—while 22,000 BTC moved to exchanges. Galaxy Research separately counted roughly 1,596 BTC stolen across more than 5,200 addresses in three attack waves, and the firmware bug had reduced key entropy from 128 bits to about 40 bits.

rss · Decrypt · Aug 13, 02:16 · Single source

Background, discussion, and references

Market impact

The incident may affect Bitcoin flows and sentiment: 22,000 BTC moved to exchanges, adding to sell-side liquidity, while long-term holders moving 233,000 BTC to safer custody could reduce future selling pressure. Hardware-wallet security sentiment could influence demand for self-custody tools and exchange balances, but the net market direction is not discernible.

Background

Coldcard is a Bitcoin-only hardware wallet made by Canadian company Coinkite, designed to keep private keys fully offline. A firmware bug introduced in March 2021 routed key generation through a weak software random number generator rather than the device's hardware secure element, making private keys guessable. Self-custody means holding one's own private keys instead of leaving Bitcoin with an exchange, and multisig wallets require multiple independent keys to authorize a transaction, so no single compromised device can drain funds.
